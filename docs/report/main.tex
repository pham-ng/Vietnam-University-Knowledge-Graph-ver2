\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Information about Vietnamese higher education institutions is scattered across Wikipedia articles, Wikidata items
and administrative documents, and it became partly obsolete when the country reorganised its provinces in July~2025.
This report presents \proj{}, an open knowledge graph of \textbf{300 Vietnamese educational institutions} (271 of them
higher education institutions), together with their 63 current and former provinces, 50 governing bodies and
1{,}695 people, published according to the \emph{five-star Linked Open Data} scheme. We designed an OWL~2~RL ontology
of 34 classes and 47 properties whose defined classes and property chains let a rule reasoner classify
institutions (public/private, military/police, member institutions) and propagate locations through the 2025 province
mergers; data-quality constraints are expressed separately in SHACL. A medallion (bronze/silver/gold) pipeline
collects data from Wikidata and Vietnamese Wikipedia, resolves entities by Wikidata identifiers, records every
conflict and fallback value, and enforces a JSON-Schema data contract before RDF generation. The resulting graph contains
39{,}507 triples (14{,}666 of them inferred), reuses standard vocabularies for 74\% of asserted triples, and links
84\% of its entities via \texttt{owl:sameAs} to Wikidata, DBpedia, ROR and GeoNames. Every one of its 2{,}241 HTTP URIs
dereferences to HTML with embedded JSON-LD, Turtle and JSON-LD, and a hardened SPARQL~1.1 endpoint supports
federated queries. Compared with a previous version of the project, \proj{} answers 21/21 benchmark facts correctly
(vs.\ 14/21), is logically consistent (0 vs.\ 349 violations), and answers aggregate and multi-hop questions that the
earlier model could not express. An empirical comparison also shows why identifier-based linking was preferred over
string-similarity linking (100\% vs.\ at most 98.3\% precision, with 66--84\% recall for the latter).
\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Linked Open Data, knowledge graph, ontology engineering, OWL 2 RL, SHACL, data integration,
owl:sameAs, Wikidata, higher education, Vietnam.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vietnam has more than 250 higher education institutions (HEIs) of very different legal types: national and regional
universities, universities, academies, officer schools, and their member schools and branches. Basic facts about them
-- founding year, governing ministry, public or private ownership, location, leadership, admission codes -- are
published in many places but rarely in a form that machines can query, compare and combine. Two recent events make the
problem more pressing. First, Resolution 202/2025/QH15 \cite{vn2025nq202} merged the 63 provinces into 34 from
1~July~2025, so the location of many institutions in existing datasets is now obsolete. Second, community knowledge
bases such as Wikidata \cite{vrandecic2014wikidata} and DBpedia \cite{lehmann2015dbpedia} cover Vietnamese institutions
only partially and inconsistently, while the richer Vietnamese Wikipedia infoboxes are semi-structured text.

The Linked Data principles \cite{bernerslee2006linked,bizer2009linked} and the five-star open data scheme provide a
well-established way to publish such information: give every entity an HTTP URI, return useful RDF when the URI is
looked up, use W3C standards, and link to other datasets. This project applies them to Vietnamese higher education
and, in doing so, addresses the weaknesses of an earlier version of the same project
\cite{phamng2025vukg,ha2025viodbpedia}, whose ontology produced 349 logical contradictions under reasoning and whose
URIs could not be dereferenced.

\paragraph{Research questions.} The work is organised around four questions:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2cm,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1] How can an ontology for Vietnamese higher education be designed so that standard rule-based reasoning is
             both \emph{correct} (no spurious contradictions) and \emph{useful} (it derives facts users ask for)?
  \item[RQ2] How can two heterogeneous community sources be integrated into accurate, traceable data, and how much
             more accurate is the result than a single-source approach?
  \item[RQ3] How should the dataset be linked to existing Linked Data -- in particular, when should existing URIs be
             reused and when should new URIs be minted?
  \item[RQ4] Does the published dataset actually satisfy all five stars, and is it usable by external people and
             programs?
\end{description}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An OWL~2~RL ontology (34 classes, 27 object and 20 datatype properties) with defined classes, four property
        chains and disjointness axioms, aligned to schema.org, FOAF and DBpedia, plus 8 SHACL shapes (\cref{sec:onto}).
  \item A reproducible bronze/silver/gold integration pipeline with an explicit conflict-resolution policy,
        provenance at the level of individual Wikipedia revisions, and a JSON-Schema data contract (\cref{sec:integration}).
  \item A URI and linking strategy that reuses existing vocabularies and IRIs wherever possible, mints URIs only for
        described entities, and links them with \texttt{owl:sameAs} after explicit verification (\cref{sec:linking}).
  \item A public, five-star publication: 2{,}241 dereferenceable URIs, a hardened SPARQL endpoint with federated
        queries, interactive maps, statistics and an ontology browser (\cref{sec:publication}).
  \item A quantitative evaluation of completeness, correctness, consistency, link quality, performance and security,
        including a comparison with the previous version and with string-similarity linking (\cref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Linked Data and the five-star scheme}
Linked Data \cite{bernerslee2006linked,heath2011linked} rests on four principles: use URIs as names for things; use HTTP
URIs so that people can look them up; return useful information in standard formats (RDF \cite{w3c2014rdf},
SPARQL \cite{w3c2013sparql}) when a URI is looked up; and include links to other URIs. The five-star scheme grades open
data from \emph{available on the Web under an open licence} (\(\star\)) to \emph{machine-readable} (\(\star\star\)),
\emph{non-proprietary format} (\(\star\star\star\)), \emph{W3C standards and URIs} (\(\star\star\star\star\)) and
\emph{linked to other data} (\(\star\star\star\star\star\)). The W3C Best Practices \cite{w3c2014bp} and Cool URIs
\cite{w3c2008cooluris} recommend reusing vocabularies, minting stable HTTP URIs in a namespace the publisher controls,
and describing datasets with VoID \cite{w3c2011void} and DCAT \cite{w3c2020dcat}. Empirical studies show that
conformance to these practices is far from universal \cite{hogan2012conformance}.

\subsection{Ontologies, reasoning and validation}
OWL~2~RL \cite{w3c2012owlprofiles} is the OWL profile designed for rule-based implementation: reasoning can be
performed by forward chaining in polynomial time, which makes it practical to \emph{materialise} all consequences and
serve them from an ordinary triple store. Because OWL follows the open-world assumption, cardinality restrictions do
not detect missing data; SHACL \cite{w3c2017shacl} closes this gap by validating RDF graphs against shapes. PROV-O
\cite{w3c2013provo} records provenance and JSON-LD \cite{w3c2020jsonld} embeds RDF into Web pages.

\subsection{Knowledge graphs extracted from Wikipedia}
DBpedia \cite{lehmann2015dbpedia} and YAGO \cite{suchanek2007yago} extract knowledge graphs from Wikipedia infoboxes and
categories; Wikidata \cite{vrandecic2014wikidata} is a collaboratively edited knowledge base that now serves as a hub of
identifiers. Hogan et al.\ \cite{hogan2021kg} survey the broader field of knowledge graphs. Schema.org
\cite{guha2016schemaorg} provides widely used classes such as \texttt{CollegeOrUniversity}.

\subsection{Identity links and link discovery}
\texttt{owl:sameAs} asserts that two URIs denote the same individual; Halpin et al.\ \cite{halpin2010sameas} showed
that it is frequently misused for mere similarity, which propagates errors under reasoning. Link discovery frameworks
such as Silk \cite{volz2009silk} generate links from similarity metrics and thresholds. Zaveri et al.\
\cite{zaveri2016quality} survey Linked Data quality dimensions, several of which (completeness, consistency,
interlinking, licensing, provenance) we measure in \cref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Prior course projects (baselines)}
\label{sec:baselines}
\emph{vio} \cite{ha2025viodbpedia,phamng2025vukg} built a Vietnamese DBpedia-style ontology and a dataset of
universities from Vietnamese Wikipedia; it is the direct predecessor of this work and serves as our main baseline.
\emph{hust-semantic-web} \cite{phuongbv2025hust} built a smartphone dataset using a medallion architecture and Silk
linking; we adopted its medallion idea and used its linking approach as a comparison point.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All HTTP responses are cached on disk, so re-running the pipeline yields identical output;
every bronze snapshot has a \texttt{.meta.json} file recording source endpoint, retrieval time, user agent and licence;
and \texttt{data/manifest.json} records, for every file in every layer, its record or triple count and SHA-256 hash.
A continuous-integration workflow runs the full test suite (100 tests) and rebuilds the published site on every commit.

\paragraph{Technology.} The pipeline is written in Python (\(\approx\)5{,}100 lines) using RDFLib, OWL-RL and pySHACL
\cite{rdflib}; Apache Jena Fuseki \cite{jenafuseki} serves SPARQL locally; Oxigraph \cite{oxigraph} compiled to
WebAssembly provides SPARQL in the browser; a Flask/Waitress server provides the public endpoint.

\section{Ontology Design}
\label{sec:onto}

\subsection{Design principles}
The ontology (namespace \texttt{vnedu:}) was designed to fix the specific failures we observed when running a rule
reasoner over the baseline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stay inside OWL~2~RL} so that materialisation is sound and complete and the closure can be served by
        any triple store.
  \item \textbf{Never redefine external vocabularies.} The baseline declared \texttt{geo:lat rdfs:domain vio:Site};
        since provinces also have coordinates, the reasoner typed them as \texttt{Site}, which was disjoint with
        \texttt{Place} -- 344 of the baseline's 349 contradictions came from this single axiom.
  \item \textbf{Align, do not equate.} Our classes are connected to schema.org, FOAF and DBpedia only through
        \texttt{rdfs:subClassOf}/\texttt{rdfs:subPropertyOf} (26 alignment axioms), never
        \texttt{owl:equivalentClass}, so external semantics cannot leak in.
  \item \textbf{Property chains range over common super-classes}, so that chaining never forces an individual into an
        incompatible class.
  \item \textbf{Constraints belong in SHACL}, because OWL's open-world semantics cannot detect missing values.
  \item \textbf{Defined classes for classification, disjointness and functional properties for error detection.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Class hierarchy}
\Cref{fig:classes} shows the class hierarchy. Institution types follow the Law on Higher Education
\cite{vn2018law34} and are determined from the institution's official name (\emph{Đại học}, \emph{Trường Đại học},
\emph{Học viện}, \emph{Trường Sĩ quan}) rather than from Wikidata's \texttt{P31}, which mixes universities, colleges and
faculties. The four legal types are pairwise disjoint (\texttt{owl:AllDisjointClasses}). Six \emph{defined classes} are
populated by the reasoner only.

\subsection{SHACL shapes}
Eight node shapes with 23 property constraints and 2 SPARQL-based constraints check what OWL cannot: language-tagged
labels in Vietnamese and English, \texttt{xsd:gYear} founding years between 1800 and the current year, admission codes
matching \texttt{\^{}[A-Z]\{3\}\$}, coordinates inside Vietnam's bounding box, exactly one region per province, member
institutions in the same province as their parent (informational), and a warning when a private institution lists a
ministry as its governing body.

\subsection{URI design}
All URIs live under the namespace controlled by the project, \base{}, with the pattern
\texttt{resource/\{type\}/\{slug\}}, where the slug is the accent-free official Vietnamese name
(e.g.\ \texttt{resource/university/dai-hoc-bach-khoa-ha-noi}). Slugs are stable (they do not encode database keys) and
human-readable; homonyms of defunct institutions receive their period of activity, e.g.\ ``Đại học Cần Thơ
(1966--1975)''. The ontology itself is published at \texttt{\ldots/ontology} with hash URIs for terms.

\subsection{Cleaning semi-structured infoboxes}
Infobox values are wikitext, not data. The parser (based on \texttt{mwparserfromhell}) handles line-break templates
whose removal would glue tokens together (\texttt{BKHN<br>HUST} \(\to\) ``BKHN | HUST''), list templates, date
templates (\texttt{\{\{start date|1956|3|6\}\}} \(\to\) \texttt{1956-03-06}), degree--minute--second coordinates,
references, invisible soft hyphens and image captions. Infobox templates with English parameter names
(\texttt{principal}, \texttt{parent}, \texttt{affiliation}) are normalised to their Vietnamese equivalents; before this
normalisation one institution (VinUni) silently lost its rector, owner, council chair and partners. Person names are
separated from academic titles and ranks (e.g.\ ``GS.TS.NGND'' \(\to\) \vn{honorific}).

\subsection{Entity resolution and scope}
Wikidata QIDs are the primary key. Missing sitelinks are repaired by title search (accepting only articles not
attached to another item); three groups of duplicate Wikidata items (e.g.\ Hanoi Law University has three QIDs) are
merged into one entity that keeps \texttt{owl:sameAs} to all of them. \textbf{68 candidate entities were excluded}, each
with a recorded reason: faculties and departments, high schools, list and history articles, and English-only items
that could not be cross-checked.

\subsection{Conflict resolution and fallbacks}
\Cref{tab:policy} states the resolution policy per field. All 42 cases where sources disagree are written to
\texttt{conflicts.csv}; every value obtained from a lower-priority fallback (176 values, mostly coordinates, governing
bodies and ownership) is written to \texttt{filled.csv} with its source, so a reviewer can audit exactly which facts
were not taken from a structured field.

Before RDF generation every silver record is validated against a JSON Schema
(file \url{schemas/silver.schema.json}) covering identifiers, enumerations, value ranges, URL and email formats, image URLs, and cross-field rules (e.g.\ a
former province must have a successor). Any violation stops the pipeline.

\section{RDF Generation and Linking}
\label{sec:linking}

\subsection{Vocabulary reuse and provenance}
RDF is generated from silver records by \texttt{step3\_transform.py}. Standard vocabularies are used wherever an
appropriate term exists -- RDFS, SKOS, FOAF, schema.org, DBpedia ontology, W3C Geo, PROV-O -- so that \textbf{74\% of
asserted triples} (15{,}083 of 20{,}446) use externally defined predicates. Every institution carries
\texttt{prov:wasDerivedFrom} links to its Wikidata item \emph{and to the exact Wikipedia revision} (\texttt{oldid}) that
was parsed, so any value can be traced to the text it came from. Textual descriptions (\texttt{dbo:abstract},
\vn{history}) are attributed to that revision under CC BY-SA.

\subsection{URI strategy: when to reuse and when to mint}
\label{sec:uristrategy}
A natural question is why the project mints its own URIs instead of using Wikidata's. Our answer follows the W3C
best practices \cite{w3c2014bp}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reuse whenever we only \emph{refer} to something.} Occupations and genders of alumni, partner
        universities and image files are referenced directly by their existing IRIs (222 Wikidata IRIs in 2{,}523
        triples), with only a label added for display. We do not mint \texttt{vnedu:occupation/politician}.
  \item \textbf{Mint only for entities we \emph{describe}.} The 2{,}241 entities we publish facts about need URIs whose
        lookup returns \emph{our} description (Linked Data principle~3): admission codes, legal type, post-2025
        province, inferred classification. A Wikidata URI cannot be made to return that. Keeping our assertions
        under our own URIs also keeps their provenance separate, covers entities Wikidata does not have (training
        majors, province-merger relations), and lets the project guarantee dereferenceability of its namespace.
  \item \textbf{Assert identity explicitly.} \textbf{84\%} of minted entities (1{,}874/2{,}241) carry
        \texttt{owl:sameAs} to existing URIs; where identity is not exact (a training \emph{major} vs.\ an academic
        \emph{discipline}) we use \texttt{skos:closeMatch} instead, following \cite{halpin2010sameas}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Identifier-based linking with verification}
Links are derived from shared identifiers rather than name similarity (\cref{tab:links}). Wikidata links come from the
data's own provenance; ROR and GeoNames links from Wikidata properties P6782 and P1566; DBpedia links from DBpedia's
own \texttt{owl:sameAs} to Wikidata. Two verification steps guard against wrong identity links: governing bodies and
regions without a QID are searched by Vietnamese label \emph{and} must satisfy \texttt{P17 = Vietnam} (this rejected,
e.g., a Korean ministry with the same name); and DBpedia resources that DBpedia maps to several Wikidata items are
rejected (14 cases, e.g.\ \texttt{dbr:Pleiku} wrongly mapped to Gia Lai province). Links are not fed to the reasoner,
because \texttt{owl:sameAs} semantics would copy every triple onto the external URIs.

\section{Reasoning and Validation}
\label{sec:reasoning}

The asserted data, ontology and VoID description (21{,}071 triples) are materialised with OWL-RL \cite{rdflib} in about
30~seconds, producing a closure of 59{,}880 triples. From the closure we keep only \emph{instance-level} triples whose
subject belongs to the dataset (14{,}666 new triples), which avoids publishing tautologies about the vocabularies.
A set of SPARQL checks then looks for \texttt{owl:Nothing} members, violations of disjointness and
\texttt{AllDifferent}, and functional-property clashes: \textbf{the dataset is consistent (0 violations)}.
SHACL validation of asserted plus inferred data \textbf{conforms} and reports 37 warnings and 1 informational result
(\cref{tab:shacl}); the warnings are genuine gaps in the open sources, deliberately not filled by guesswork.

\subsection{Securing a public SPARQL endpoint}
An open endpoint executes arbitrary user queries, so we audited the server and reproduced two real vulnerabilities in
the first version: a \textbf{server-side request forgery} (a query with \texttt{SERVICE <http://127.0.0.1:\ldots>} made
the server issue requests to internal addresses) and a \textbf{SPARQL injection} through the resource path. The
hardened server only allows \texttt{SERVICE} calls to an allow-list (Wikidata, DBpedia), rejects variable endpoints and
\texttt{FROM} clauses, validates resource paths against the dataset's URI pattern before building queries, limits
query length (20{,}000 characters) and execution time (30~s), returns generic error messages, and sets security
headers. A further practical issue was that RDFLib hard-codes a User-Agent that Wikimedia rate-limits; the server
replaces it with a descriptive one so that federated queries work. All of these behaviours are covered by tests.

\subsection{Link quality}
\paragraph{Identifier-based vs.\ string-similarity linking.} To justify the linking strategy we simulated a Silk-style
\cite{volz2009silk} matcher -- Jaccard similarity over lower-cased tokens of English names, best candidate above a

the threshold used by the hust-semantic-web baseline (\(\theta=0.2\)) one link in six is wrong; raising \(\theta\) buys
precision at a steep cost in recall. The root cause is structural: Vietnamese university names share most tokens.
\emph{Hanoi University of Science and Technology} and \emph{University of Science and Technology of Hanoi} are two
different institutions with \emph{identical} token sets (Jaccard \(=1.0\)); no threshold separates them, whereas shared
identifiers (QID, ROR) do.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Lessons learned.}
(1) \emph{Most reasoning failures were design failures, not data failures}: a single domain declaration on an
external property caused 98\% of the baseline's contradictions. Keeping the ontology in OWL~2~RL, aligning instead of
equating, and moving completeness checks to SHACL removed all of them. (2) \emph{Two independent sources are worth more
than one large source}: most baseline errors were values that one source states and another contradicts; logging
conflicts makes these visible. (3) \emph{For identity, identifiers beat similarity}: with name-similarity matching the
best achievable F1 on our data was 0.867, while identifiers gave exact links. (4) \emph{Publishing is engineering}:
the first public endpoint had an SSRF and an injection vulnerability, and federated queries failed because of a
third-party User-Agent policy; neither was visible until the system was tested as an external client would use it.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Coverage is bounded by the open sources.} There is no machine-readable official register of
        Vietnamese HEIs, so completeness cannot be proven; private institutions (35 identified) are probably
        under-represented, and 24 HEIs have unknown ownership.
  \item \textbf{Sparse quantitative attributes:} students (9\%), admission codes (20\%), staff counts.
  \item \textbf{Programmes are sample data:} 37 majors and 78 programmes were curated by hand for 16 institutions;
        queries about ``who trains major X'' are illustrative, not statistics.
  \item \textbf{Limited temporal modelling:} leader terms, dated student counts and renaming events are not modelled
        as time-indexed facts.
  \item \textbf{Hosting:} primary URIs are on static hosting; content negotiation and the SPARQL endpoint run on a
        free-tier server that sleeps when idle and keeps the graph in memory, which suits a dataset of this size but
        not heavy load. Timed-out queries cannot be forcibly stopped by rdflib.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
The correctness benchmark is small (21 facts) and biased towards well-known institutions, so it demonstrates the kind
of errors avoided rather than estimating an error rate for the whole dataset; a random sample of about 50 institutions
checked against official websites would give such an estimate. Wikipedia and Wikidata are community-edited and may be
outdated (e.g.\ leaders); revision-level provenance makes every value auditable but does not make it correct.
Server latencies were measured on a shared free-tier instance and vary with load.

% ================================================================= 11
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\proj{} shows that a small team can publish a genuinely five-star dataset by combining careful ontology design,
multi-source integration with explicit provenance and quality gates, identifier-based linking, and an engineering
approach to publication. The resulting knowledge graph of 300 institutions and 39{,}507 triples is logically
consistent, answers all benchmark facts correctly, supports aggregate, multi-hop and federated questions, and is usable
by external clients through dereferenceable URIs and a public SPARQL endpoint.

Future work includes: integrating the Ministry of Education and Training's official lists and admission data to
measure and improve coverage; modelling time-indexed facts (leadership terms, enrolment by year, renaming and merger
events) with an n-ary or RDF-star pattern; replacing the sample programme data with programme catalogues; registering
a persistent namespace (e.g.\ \texttt{w3id.org/vnedu}) with server-side content negotiation; and contributing verified
corrections (e.g.\ missing sitelinks and ambiguous DBpedia mappings found in this work) back to Wikidata.

\section{Example federated query}
\begin{code}
# HEI density per province: our data + area fetched from Wikidata through owl:sameAs
PREFIX vnedu: <https://pham-ng.github.io/Vietnam-University-Knowledge-Graph-ver2/ontology#>
PREFIX rdfs:  <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>
PREFIX owl:   <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#>
PREFIX wdt:   <http://www.wikidata.org/prop/direct/>
SELECT ?tinh (COUNT(DISTINCT ?u) AS ?so_co_so) ?dien_tich_km2 WHERE {
  ?u a vnedu:HigherEducationInstitution ; vnedu:locatedIn ?p .
  ?p a vnedu:Province ; rdfs:label ?tinh ; owl:sameAs ?wd .
  FILTER(LANG(?tinh) = "vi" && STRSTARTS(STR(?wd), "http://www.wikidata.org/entity/"))
  OPTIONAL { SERVICE <https://query.wikidata.org/sparql> { ?wd wdt:P2046 ?dien_tich_km2 } }
} GROUP BY ?tinh ?dien_tich_km2 ORDER BY DESC(?so_co_so)
\end{code}
